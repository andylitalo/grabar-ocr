## English

These [are ordered] because they do not succeed each other in the same manner year after year.

Fifth: From the first Sunday after the Nativity until the Sunday of the Eve of mid-Fast [Median Autumn / Mid-Lent], however many Sundays may occur, for their evening Resurrection Hampartsi [hymns of Ascension/Dismissal] — of whatever tone they may be — you must say the "Ter Herknits" [Praise the Lord from the heavens] unto the very end, and conclude [thus] in every yearly calendar. But on other Sundays where it is written "to the end," there also you must say it to the end; and where "to the end" is not placed, you must say only that which is placed.

Sixth: The feasts of the Saints for the most part are set down plurally in our Tonatsuyts [Directory] from its original state, that is, two, three, four, five, and others, many or few, which are always celebrated together indivisibly, as you see. These we have likewise placed in the First Volume as before. But when we mention such in this Second Volume, we have placed only the name of the first saints in many places for the sake of brevity; nevertheless, when you encounter such feasts in this Second Volume, you must celebrate, along with the first-named saint, all the other companions following him, and commemorate their names by that canon as they are set down in the First Volume.

Seventh: In our Tonatsuyts, the feasts which frequently shift and are celebrated in various and different intervals are these:

The feasts written after the Nativity with penance;

The feasts after the second week of Vardavar [Transfiguration];

The last feasts of the fourth week of the Assumption of the Mother of God, namely: Andrew the General and Adrian; and the very last two feasts, namely: Abraham and Khoren, and Astuatsatur [Adeodatus]. Now, I have mentioned these for this reason, so that you may mark them and know their places, so that wherever you may celebrate them, you shall see their exemplars in their proper place.
